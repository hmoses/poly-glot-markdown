🔍 Why RAG & GEO Matter

📚

RAG — Retrieval-Augmented Generation

RAG is how AI systems find and use your content. When someone asks ChatGPT, Copilot, or an internal AI assistant a question, RAG is the pipeline that retrieves relevant documents, chunks them, and feeds them to the language model.

The problem: Most documentation is written for humans, not machines. AI retrieval systems split documents into chunks — and poorly structured docs produce bad chunks. Missing frontmatter means no metadata filtering. No heading hierarchy means chunks split mid-thought. No summaries means the AI has to guess what each section is about.
What Poly-Glot fixes:
  • Adds YAML frontmatter so vector databases can filter by title, tags, and date
  • Enforces heading hierarchy (H1 → H2 → H3) so chunkers split at semantic boundaries
  • Inserts section summaries so the most important info appears at the top of each chunk — where LLMs pay the most attention (Liu et al. 2023)
  • Adds explicit chunk boundaries (<!-- chunk-boundary -->) to prevent mid-sentence splits
  • Generates citation anchors ({#section-slug}) for passage-level retrieval
↑ 40% Retrieval accuracy improvement from structured chunking vs. naive splitting (Pinecone, 2023)
🌐

GEO — Generative Engine Optimization

GEO is the new SEO. When users search with AI engines like Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, ChatGPT Search, or Gemini — those engines cite sources. GEO determines whether your content gets cited or someone else's.

The problem: Traditional SEO optimizes for link ranking. But AI search engines don't show ten blue links — they generate answers and cite sources inline. If your content lacks authoritative signals (clear titles, bolded key phrases, structured data, freshness dates), AI engines will cite your competitors instead.
What Poly-Glot fixes:
  • Generates descriptive titles and meta descriptions that AI engines use for summarization
  • Bolds 3–5 key phrases per section — the exact pattern that increases AI citation likelihood (Aggarwal et al. 2023)
  • Extracts and surfaces topical keywords/tags for semantic matching
  • Adds author attribution and freshness dates (last_reviewed, expires) for trust scoring
  • Structures content with clear Q&A patterns that AI engines prefer to cite
↑ 20–40% Visibility improvement from keyword optimization and authoritative language (GEO paper, Princeton/Georgia Tech 2023)

Instant Mode — What It Does

Seven deterministic rules run entirely in your browser with zero latency. No network calls, no API keys, works offline. Every transformation is predictable and repeatable.

  • 🏷️ Frontmatter Generation Detects missing YAML frontmatter and generates it from document content — title extracted from H1, description from first paragraph, tags from keyword frequency analysis, plus last_reviewed, expires (90 days), source, and version fields. If frontmatter exists, enriches it with any missing fields without overwriting existing values.
  • 📐 Heading Hierarchy Fix Enforces single-H1 rule — demotes duplicate H1s to H2. Scans for heading-level jumps (H1 → H3) that break semantic chunking in vector databases. Ensures clean H1 → H2 → H3 progression that tools like LlamaIndex and LangChain rely on for recursive splitting.
  • 🔗 Citation Anchor IDs Adds {#section-slug} anchor IDs to every H2 and H3 heading. Enables passage-level retrieval — RAG systems can link directly to a specific section instead of returning the entire document. Slugs are auto-generated from heading text (lowercase, hyphenated).
  • 🎯 GEO Keyword Bolding Extracts the top keywords using word frequency + bigram analysis with stopword filtering. Bolds up to 5 high-value phrases using **keyword** syntax. Skips headings, code blocks, and frontmatter. This is the single highest-impact GEO optimization — bolded key phrases increase AI search citation by 20–40% (Aggarwal et al. 2023).
  • ✂️ RAG Chunk Boundaries Inserts <!-- chunk-boundary --> markers between major sections (before each H2). Prevents vector database chunkers from splitting mid-paragraph or mid-thought. Explicit boundaries give you control over where chunks break instead of relying on token-count splitting.
  • 📝 Section Summaries Generates a > **Summary:** ... blockquote after each H2 heading, extracted from the first 200 characters of section content. Places the most important information at the top of each chunk — critical because LLMs attend most to the beginning of retrieved passages (Liu et al. "Lost in the Middle" 2023).
  • 🔑 Keyword Extraction Engine Strips frontmatter and code blocks, tokenizes text, filters 80+ English stopwords, counts unigram and bigram frequencies (bigrams boosted 1.5×), and returns the top 10 terms. Used by both frontmatter tag generation and GEO bolding. Entirely client-side — your content never leaves the browser.

AI-Enhanced Mode — What It Does

Sends your document to Google Gemini 3.6 Flash via the Poly-Glot server for deep semantic rewriting. The AI understands your content's meaning — not just its structure — and produces naturally written, publication-ready output.

  • 🧠 Semantic Rewriting Gemini reads and understands your entire document, then rewrites vague or ambiguous sentences for clarity. Converts run-on paragraphs into structured, scannable text. Preserves your voice and tone while making every sentence precise enough for AI retrieval.
  • 🏷️ Intelligent Frontmatter Generates contextually accurate metadata — the AI writes a real description (not just the first sentence), selects semantically relevant tags, and adds freshness fields (last_reviewed, expires, source, version). Enriches existing frontmatter without overwriting.
  • 📝 Natural Summaries Instead of extracting the first 200 characters, the AI writes a genuine summary for each H2 section that captures the key takeaway — the kind of summary a human editor would write. These summaries become the most-attended text in each RAG chunk.
  • 🎯 Context-Aware GEO Optimization The AI identifies which phrases actually matter for search visibility based on semantic understanding, not just word frequency. Bolds the phrases that AI search engines (Perplexity, ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews) are most likely to cite.
  • 📐 Intelligent Restructuring Fixes heading hierarchy, splits oversized sections (>500 words) into logical sub-sections, merges tiny fragments, and adds anchor IDs — all while maintaining logical flow. The AI understands where a new section should start based on topic shifts, not just word count.
  • ✂️ Smart Chunk Optimization Places chunk boundaries at natural topic transitions. Adds summary blockquotes and citation anchors. Targets 100–500 words per chunk — the sweet spot for embedding models like text-embedding-3-small and voyage-3.
  • 🧩 MDX Preservation When MDX mode is enabled, the AI preserves all JSX components (<Callout>, <Tab>, <CodeBlock>) and adds descriptive comments above each component explaining its purpose — making MDX files both human-readable and AI-parseable.

📐 How Scoring Works

Scores are structural heuristics grounded in published RAG and GEO research. They measure how well your document follows patterns proven to improve retrieval quality and AI search visibility.

RAG Score (0–100)

Measures retrieval-readiness — how well a vector search system can chunk, index, and retrieve your document.

  • Frontmatter metadata (20pts) — title, description, tags improve chunk metadata filtering Pinecone, 2023
  • Heading hierarchy (20pts) — single H1, logical H2/H3 enables semantic chunking LlamaIndex RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter
  • Section summaries (20pts) — dense passage-initial info improves retrieval Liu et al. "Lost in the Middle", Stanford 2023
  • Chunk boundaries (5pts) — explicit markers prevent mid-sentence splits LangChain chunking docs
  • Citation anchors (5pts) — heading IDs enable passage-level retrieval Google Passage Indexing
  • Freshness metadata (5pts) — last_reviewed, expires prevent stale retrieval Google Search Quality Guidelines
  • Content signals (25pts) — code blocks, lists, tables, links, document length

GEO Score (0–100)

Measures AI search visibility — how likely AI engines (Perplexity, ChatGPT, Gemini) are to cite your document.

  • Authoritative title (20pts) — clear, descriptive title >10 chars Aggarwal et al. "GEO", 2023
  • Description (15pts) — meta description for AI summarization
  • Tags & keywords (23pts) — topical signals for semantic matching GEO paper: keyword optimization +20-40% visibility
  • Bold emphasis (18pts) — key phrases bolded for AI extraction GEO paper: authoritative language increases citation
  • Author & date (9pts) — authorship signals for trust scoring
  • Freshness (5pts) — last_reviewed and expires dates
  • Content depth (10pts) — sufficient word count for comprehensive answers
